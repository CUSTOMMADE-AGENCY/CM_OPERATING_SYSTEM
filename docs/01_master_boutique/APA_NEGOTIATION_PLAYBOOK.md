# APA Negotiation Playbook

## Purpose

This playbook defines how CM manages the business process for negotiating an asset purchase agreement for catalog, master, publishing or other music-rights transactions. It ensures that deal terms, diligence findings, risk allocation, approvals, closing deliverables and post-closing obligations are tracked with discipline.

The goal is to avoid negotiating long-form legal language without a clear business position. Every material APA issue should have an owner, risk level, deadline, recommended position and approval path.

## Scope

This playbook applies after a deal has moved from initial interest into an LOI, term sheet, accepted business framework or long-form APA process. It covers:

- Business-point preparation.
- Counsel coordination.
- Issue-log management.
- Diligence issue allocation.
- Economic and risk-position escalation.
- Closing checklist management.
- Post-closing handoff.

This playbook does not replace legal advice. Counsel owns legal drafting, legal interpretation and legal risk analysis. The deal lead owns business coordination, client alignment, issue tracking and negotiation execution.

## Owner

The deal lead owns the APA negotiation process, business issue log, timeline, internal coordination and client-facing business recommendation.

Counsel owns legal drafting, legal comments, enforceability analysis, legal risk advice and final legal document review.

The client or designated decision owner approves material economics, liability exposure, title positions, closing conditions, indemnity positions and concessions.

Finance, royalty, tax, accounting, rights and operations contributors own the specialist inputs assigned to them.

## Inputs

Before negotiating the APA, gather:

- Executed LOI, term sheet or approved business framework.
- Valuation memo and approved negotiation range.
- Diligence checklist and current diligence issue log.
- Asset schedules and excluded asset list.
- Ownership summaries and chain-of-title materials.
- Royalty statements, source reports and revenue support.
- Known claims, disputes, liens, consents, notices, reversions and recoupment items.
- Buyer or seller markup and counsel comments.
- Tax, accounting, payment and entity information.
- Required closing deliverables.
- Approval thresholds for price, holdback, indemnity, reps, warranties and closing conditions.
- Target signing date, target closing date and outside date if applicable.

## Negotiation principles

- Business terms must be aligned before legal language is treated as final.
- Material deviations from the LOI must be identified and approved.
- Diligence issues must be resolved, allocated or accepted; they should not disappear from the issue log without explanation.
- Economic concessions, liability exposure and title risk require decision-owner approval.
- Counsel should receive clear business instructions, not vague preferences.
- Closing readiness must be tracked in parallel with document negotiation.
- Post-closing obligations must be assigned before signing.

## Negotiation areas

Track the following areas in every APA negotiation unless clearly not applicable:

- Purchased assets.
- Excluded assets.
- Purchase price.
- Payment timing.
- Holdbacks, escrows or earnouts.
- Assumed liabilities and excluded liabilities.
- Representations and warranties.
- Disclosure schedules.
- Indemnities, caps, baskets and survival periods.
- Consents, notices and third-party approvals.
- Conditions precedent.
- Closing deliverables.
- Royalty accounting and post-closing collections.
- Transition assistance.
- Tax forms and payment instructions.
- Confidentiality, announcements and communications.
- Termination rights and outside dates.

## Process steps

### 1. Confirm deal baseline

- Review the LOI or approved business framework.
- Identify agreed economics, asset perimeter, timing and risk allocation.
- Confirm open items that were deferred to long-form documentation.
- Identify internal approval thresholds.
- Confirm whether CM is representing buyer, seller or another stakeholder.

### 2. Build the business-point checklist

Create a checklist that compares the APA draft to the agreed framework. The checklist should include:

- Purchase price and payment terms.
- Assets included and excluded.
- Closing timeline.
- Consents and notices.
- Diligence exceptions.
- Reps and warranties.
- Indemnity structure.
- Holdbacks or escrows.
- Post-closing obligations.

### 3. Create the issue log

Each issue must include:

- Issue description.
- Agreement section or schedule.
- Business owner.
- Legal owner, if applicable.
- Risk level.
- Financial impact, if known.
- Recommended position.
- Current counterparty position.
- Next action.
- Due date.
- Status.

### 4. Align with counsel

- Provide counsel with the LOI, issue log, asset schedules and diligence issues.
- Identify which points require legal drafting versus business decision-making.
- Confirm counsel's deadline for markup or response.
- Ask counsel to flag legal issues that require client approval.
- Keep privileged legal advice in the appropriate communication channel.

### 5. Review asset schedules

- Confirm that purchased assets match the deal perimeter.
- Confirm excluded assets are clearly listed.
- Check titles, identifiers, ownership percentages and related income streams.
- Flag missing metadata or uncertain title.
- Confirm whether schedules need update before signing or closing.

### 6. Allocate diligence issues

For each diligence issue, decide whether it should be:

- Resolved before signing.
- Resolved before closing.
- Covered by a representation.
- Covered by a covenant.
- Covered by indemnity.
- Covered by holdback or escrow.
- Excluded from purchased assets.
- Accepted as a known risk.

The decision must be documented in the issue log.

### 7. Negotiate economics and risk allocation

Escalate and obtain approval before accepting changes to:

- Purchase price.
- Payment timing.
- Holdback or escrow amount.
- Earnout terms.
- Indemnity cap or basket.
- Survival period.
- Fundamental representation scope.
- Title risk.
- Assumed liabilities.
- Closing conditions.

### 8. Track consents, notices and closing deliverables

- Identify all required third-party consents.
- Identify notice obligations.
- Confirm tax forms and payment instructions.
- Confirm signature authority.
- Track deliverables by owner and deadline.
- Confirm whether any item is a signing condition, closing condition or post-closing covenant.

### 9. Manage communication

- Keep the client updated on material issues and upcoming decisions.
- Send counsel consolidated business instructions.
- Avoid negotiating legal language directly without counsel where legal risk is involved.
- Confirm concessions in writing.
- Keep external communications consistent with the approved negotiation position.

### 10. Confirm signing readiness

Before signing, confirm:

- Final APA matches approved business terms.
- Schedules are complete or appropriately qualified.
- Disclosure schedules are reviewed.
- Required approvals are documented.
- Signature packets are complete.
- Closing deliverables and funds flow are confirmed.
- Post-closing obligations have owners.

### 11. Manage closing and handoff

After signing or closing, confirm:

- Signed documents are stored in the approved workspace.
- Funds flow and payment confirmations are tracked.
- Notices and payor changes are sent where required.
- Royalty collection transition is assigned.
- Post-closing covenants are calendared.
- Any holdback, escrow, indemnity or true-up obligations are tracked.

## Outputs

A complete APA negotiation process should produce:

- Business-point checklist.
- APA issue log.
- Diligence allocation tracker.
- Closing checklist.
- Approved negotiation positions.
- Counsel instruction notes.
- Client approval record.
- Final executed APA and schedules stored outside the repository.
- Post-closing obligation tracker.

## Quality checks

Before signing, confirm:

- The APA reflects the approved LOI or all deviations are documented and approved.
- Purchased and excluded assets are clear.
- Schedules are complete enough for closing or the gap is intentionally allocated.
- Material diligence exceptions are resolved, allocated or accepted.
- Economic concessions are approved.
- Liability exposure is approved.
- Counsel has reviewed legal language.
- Closing deliverables have owners and deadlines.
- Funds flow and payment instructions are confirmed.
- Post-closing obligations are assigned.
- Signed agreements and confidential schedules are not committed to the repository.

## Escalation rules

Escalate immediately when:

- Counterparty language changes agreed economics.
- Asset ownership, title or transferability is unclear.
- A diligence issue could reduce value or create liability.
- Indemnity, cap, basket or survival terms exceed approved risk tolerance.
- Payment timing or funds flow changes.
- A required consent may not arrive before signing or closing.
- A closing condition cannot be satisfied on time.
- Counsel identifies legal risk requiring business approval.

Each escalation must state the issue, affected agreement section, risk, recommendation, deadline and decision owner.

## Review cadence

Review this playbook at the start of every APA negotiation. Update the issue log during each markup round and before every negotiation call. Review the closing checklist at least twice weekly during the final signing or closing period, or daily when closing is imminent.

Review this playbook after each completed transaction and semiannually with the deal team or counsel to incorporate lessons learned.

## Repository hygiene

- Do not commit signed contracts, confidential client files, exports, duplicate folders or ad hoc drafts.
- Use clear version history through commits and pull requests instead of `final_final` filenames.
- Keep client-specific details in the appropriate live workspace, not in this repository.
