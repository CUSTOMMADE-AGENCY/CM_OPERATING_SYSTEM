# Catalog Valuation Playbook

## Purpose

Create a disciplined valuation view before outreach, LOI or negotiation.

## Scope

This playbook applies to catalog, master, publishing and neighboring-rights transactions where CM needs an internal valuation range, buyer strategy or negotiation position. It supports business judgment and deal planning; it is not a formal appraisal, tax opinion or legal conclusion.

## Owner

The deal lead owns the valuation process and recommendation. Finance, rights, royalty and legal advisors own specialist inputs and assumptions assigned to them. The final decision owner approves the valuation range before it is used externally.

## Inputs

- Historical royalty statements.
- Revenue by composition, master, territory and platform where available.
- Repertoire and metadata.
- Ownership splits and term details.
- Known claims, disputes, recoupment or administration constraints.
- Contract summaries and chain-of-title materials.
- Recent buyer feedback, comparable processes and market context.

## Approach

1. Normalize trailing revenue.
2. Identify recurring, one-off and declining income.
3. Assess concentration by work, platform, territory and counterparty.
4. Apply market multiple ranges by asset type and risk profile.
5. Create low, base and high cases.
6. Document assumptions and unresolved diligence questions.

## Process steps

1. Confirm the asset perimeter, seller objective and valuation use case.
2. Gather royalty statements, repertoire schedules, agreement summaries and metadata.
3. Reconcile reported revenue to available source reports and flag missing periods or payors.
4. Normalize trailing twelve-month and multi-year revenue for timing, anomalies and non-recurring items.
5. Segment income by asset type, territory, platform, payor, work and concentration risk.
6. Review ownership, term, reversion, consent, recoupment and dispute constraints.
7. Build low, base and high valuation cases with clear multiple and cash-flow assumptions.
8. Convert diligence issues into price, structure, holdback or representation considerations.
9. Prepare a valuation memo and obtain decision-owner approval before external use.

## Outputs

- Valuation memo with range, key assumptions, risks and recommended buyer strategy.
- Revenue normalization workbook or supporting analysis.
- Diligence issue log tied to valuation impact.
- Approved internal walk-away, target and stretch positions where applicable.

## Quality checks

- The valuation range can be traced back to source statements and assumptions.
- One-off, declining, disputed or non-recurring revenue is clearly identified.
- Ownership, term, reversion and consent issues are not treated as clean assets.
- Concentration risks are reflected in the narrative and valuation range.
- Buyer-facing numbers are approved before outreach or negotiation.
- Confidential financial materials remain in the approved data room or client workspace.

## Review cadence

Review the valuation before every outreach launch, LOI response, counteroffer and material diligence update. Refresh this playbook semiannually or when market multiples, buyer behavior or diligence standards materially change.

- Use clear version history through commits and pull requests instead of `final_final` filenames.
- Keep client-specific details in the appropriate live workspace, not in this repository.
