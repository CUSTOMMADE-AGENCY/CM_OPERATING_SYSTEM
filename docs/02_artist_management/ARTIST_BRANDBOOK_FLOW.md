# Artist Brandbook Flow

## Purpose

Turn brand strategy into a usable reference for the artist, team and external collaborators.

## Scope

This flow applies when an artist needs a practical brandbook for positioning, messaging, visuals, content direction, release communication or partner alignment. It covers discovery, drafting, review, approval and distribution of the brandbook.

It does not replace campaign-specific creative direction, final artwork approvals or legal clearance for names, marks, samples or third-party assets.

## Owner

The brand strategy owner or artist manager owns the brandbook process. The artist approves identity, tone and public-facing language. Designers, photographers, marketers and other collaborators contribute assigned inputs.

## Inputs

- Artist intake, brand audit and business audit notes.
- Existing bios, press language, visuals, artwork, EPKs and social profiles.
- Reference artists, visual references and audience insights.
- Release plans, content pillars and commercial positioning.
- Known restrictions, sensitivities or topics to avoid.
- Approved collaborator roles and feedback deadlines.

## Brandbook components

- Positioning statement.
- Artist story and tone of voice.
- Visual references and usage guidance.
- Approved bio variants.
- Social content pillars.
- Release communication principles.
- Do/don't examples.
- Audience, collaborator and partner notes where relevant.

## Process steps

1. Confirm the intended use of the brandbook and the stakeholders who need access.
2. Collect current artist materials, public profiles, release context and reference examples.
3. Identify the artist's positioning, audience, values, tone and visual direction.
4. Draft the core narrative, bio variants, message pillars and usage guidelines.
5. Build visual guidance with references, examples and practical guardrails.
6. Review the draft with the artist and capture required changes in writing.
7. Confirm final approval for core identity, tone, public-facing language and distribution list.
8. Store the approved brandbook in Google Drive and link execution tasks in ClickUp.
9. Share the approved version with collaborators and mark older drafts as superseded.

## Approval

The artist approves core identity, tone and public-facing language before the brandbook is distributed. External collaborator feedback may inform revisions, but it does not override artist approval unless the approved scope says otherwise.

## Outputs

- Approved artist brandbook.
- Approved short, medium and long bio variants where needed.
- Message pillars, tone guidance and do/don't examples.
- Visual reference guidance for collaborators.
- Distribution note identifying who may use the brandbook and for what purpose.

## Quality checks

- The brandbook is practical enough for a collaborator to use without a separate briefing.
- Public-facing language matches artist-approved positioning.
- Visual references are guidance, not unlicensed assets for reuse.
- Drafts and obsolete versions are clearly labeled.
- Sensitive topics, exclusions and approval requirements are documented.
- Brandbook access is limited to approved collaborators and team members.

## System of record

- GitHub stores this workflow, governance, SOP and system documentation.
- Google Drive stores live client folders, signed documents, deliverables and working files.
- ClickUp stores execution, pipeline stages, owners, due dates and task evidence.
- Gmail stores correspondence and approved communication templates.

## Review cadence

Review the brandbook after major releases, campaign pivots, visual rebrands, press cycles or at least twice per year for active management clients.

## Repository hygiene

- Do not commit signed contracts, confidential client files, exports, duplicate folders or ad hoc drafts.
- Use clear version history through commits and pull requests instead of `final_final` filenames.
- Keep client-specific details in the appropriate live workspace, not in this repository.
