# Monthly Management Flow

## Purpose

This flow defines the monthly operating rhythm for active artist management clients. It ensures that priorities, decisions, deadlines, blockers, approvals, risks and completed work are reviewed consistently and translated into clear next actions.

A successful monthly management cycle gives the artist a single operating picture for the next month and gives the management team a clean task structure for execution.

## Scope

This flow applies to active artist management retainers and management projects that require recurring review. It covers preparation, monthly review, decision capture, task updates, reporting and follow-up.

This flow may be used alongside weekly priority reviews, release sprint planning, artist business audits, brandbook work, deal support, rights reviews and close-out processes. It does not create a separate workflow or folder structure.

## Owner

The artist manager owns the monthly management cycle, meeting preparation, agenda, follow-up summary, ClickUp task updates and escalation list.

The artist owns priority decisions, creative approvals, commercial approvals and personal availability commitments.

Workstream owners are responsible for providing current status, completion evidence, blockers and next actions before the monthly review.

## Inputs

Before the monthly review, collect or update:

- Previous monthly management summary.
- Current artist roadmap.
- ClickUp task list with completed, active, overdue and blocked tasks.
- Weekly priority notes or release sprint notes.
- Release, content, touring, sync, publishing, brand, catalog or partnership timelines.
- Open artist approvals.
- Open advisor items from legal, accounting, distribution, publishing, business affairs or marketing.
- Budget, invoice, expense, commission or payment items where relevant.
- New opportunities, inbound requests or commercial pipeline notes.
- Known risks, scope changes or capacity constraints.
- Artist availability for the next month.

## Monthly cycle

### Preparation

The artist manager prepares a monthly pre-read before the review. The pre-read should include completed work, open priorities, decisions needed, blockers, risk items and proposed next-month priorities.

### Review

The monthly review confirms what happened, what changed and what matters next. The review should focus on decisions and tradeoffs, not only status reporting.

### Follow-up

After the review, the artist manager updates tasks, sends the monthly summary, files relevant materials and escalates specialist items.

## Process steps

### 1. Review the previous month

- Compare last month's priorities to completed work.
- Identify what was completed, delayed, canceled or changed.
- Record why any priority moved or remained blocked.
- Confirm whether deferred items still matter.

### 2. Review active workstreams

For each active workstream, confirm:

- Current status.
- Owner.
- Next action.
- Due date.
- Dependencies.
- Decision needed.
- Evidence required to close the work.

### 3. Review artist goals and constraints

- Confirm whether the artist's goals have changed.
- Confirm current creative priorities.
- Confirm availability, travel, production, family, health or business constraints that affect timing.
- Adjust roadmap sequencing if capacity or priorities have changed.

### 4. Review rights, royalties and deals

When relevant, review:

- Open contract questions.
- Royalty statement or payment issues.
- Split, ownership, metadata or collection problems.
- Publishing, distribution, label, sync, brand or catalog opportunities.
- Advisor follow-up required from counsel, accountant, business affairs or royalty specialist.

Do not resolve specialist questions by assumption. Assign them to the correct advisor or decision owner.

### 5. Review budget and commercial items

When relevant, confirm:

- Approved spend.
- Pending invoices.
- Upcoming expenses.
- Commissionable income or commercial pipeline.
- Payment timing.
- Budget risks.
- Whether any spend requires written approval before commitment.

### 6. Confirm next-month priorities

- Select the highest-impact priorities for the next month.
- Confirm what will not be prioritized.
- Identify decision deadlines.
- Confirm owner and due date for each priority.
- Check that workload fits the approved scope and available capacity.

### 7. Update execution systems

- Update ClickUp task statuses.
- Add new tasks for approved priorities.
- Close completed tasks with evidence.
- Move deferred tasks to the correct future status or backlog.
- Add links to source documents, approvals or deliverables.

### 8. Send monthly summary

The monthly summary must include:

- Completed work.
- Active priorities.
- Decisions made.
- Decisions needed.
- Risks and blockers.
- Next actions by owner.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Items deferred or out of scope.

### 9. Escalate and file

- Send specialist questions to the correct advisor.
- File deliverables and final documents in Google Drive.
- Keep approval correspondence in Gmail or linked task records.
- Update the roadmap after decisions are confirmed.

## Outputs

Each monthly cycle should produce:

- Monthly management summary.
- Updated artist roadmap.
- Updated ClickUp board.
- Current priority list for the next month.
- Decision log updates.
- Risk and blocker list.
- Advisor escalation list, if applicable.
- Filed deliverables and approval evidence.

## Quality checks

Before closing the monthly cycle, confirm:

- Every next-month priority has an owner, due date and next action.
- The artist knows which decisions are needed and by when.
- Overdue tasks are either resolved, reassigned, rescheduled or escalated.
- Deferred work is intentionally parked and not left ambiguous.
- Specialist issues are routed to qualified owners.
- Budget and commercial commitments have written approval where required.
- The monthly summary matches the updated task board.
- The roadmap reflects current artist priorities.
- No confidential files or client-specific terms are stored in the repository.

## System of record

- GitHub stores this flow and operating documentation.
- Google Drive stores client deliverables, live working files, signed documents, financial documents and monthly summary files when appropriate.
- ClickUp stores tasks, owners, statuses, due dates, blockers and completion evidence.
- Gmail stores artist approvals, external correspondence and advisor communications.

## Review cadence

Run this flow every calendar month for active management clients. If the client is in a high-intensity release period, run additional weekly or sprint reviews without replacing the monthly summary.

Review this flow quarterly to confirm that it still fits the management offer, client reporting expectations, ClickUp structure and team capacity.

## Repository hygiene

- Do not commit signed contracts, confidential client files, exports, duplicate folders or ad hoc drafts.
- Use clear version history through commits and pull requests instead of `final_final` filenames.
- Keep client-specific details in the appropriate live workspace, not in this repository.
